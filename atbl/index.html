ATBL—TurningEnterpriseComplexityintoaUsableProduct

Role Transformation and Innovation lead; product design and programming

Period 2025–present

A commercial transaction sits on top of specifications, inventory, customer pricing, taxes, approvals, ERP processes, documentation, and relationships. The design question was which complexity users need to see — and which complexity the system should absorb for them.

One decision, followed through: shipping

What I saw Orders were checked on paper. Labels were typed in Word, printed, and cut out by hand. Signed delivery receipts went into paper folders, filed by month and year; when a customer asked for one, finding it could take hours.

What I decided Put the whole path on the phone and let the order in the ERP be the only source. Items are scanned against it, labels print from it, and the driver photographs the signed receipt at the door.

What happened We estimate that dispatching goods now takes at least half the time it did. Every receipt sits in a digital archive, attached to its invoice, and when a customer asks for proof we send a link.

The system behind the experience

Designing for technical B2B

More than 26,000 active products require structured images, dimensions, attributes, standards, and technical metadata before an interface can make them meaningfully searchable. I designed and built products integrated with Sankhya ERP, keeping product decisions grounded in real inventory, pricing, and operational rules.

From interface to operating model

Digital channels handle first-level service, stock and price checks, and order tracking; technical salespeople remain focused on engineering solutions and strategic negotiations. The company recorded a 12.5% revenue increase alongside these operational changes; I treat that as business context, not as a claim that interface design alone caused growth.

What is running today

The apps share one design system and the same foundations, and they read from and write to the ERP.
